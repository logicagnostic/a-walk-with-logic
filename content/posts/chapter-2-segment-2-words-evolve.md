INT. CHURCH IN THE AGNOSTIC MOUNT - LATE MORNING

*The organ swells with energy. The crowd bubbles. The brushes skim the snare as the beat slides into place. The groove settles and the small congregation leans in as Deacon Denial steps forward to welcome them.*

Logic stakes her usual spot on the side, a quiet vantage point to observe the humans in their moment. Her ears are piqued as she takes in the sights and sounds of Deacon Denial and the congregation.

**Deacon Denial**

Welcome people. Welcome all! Praise be to people. Praise people. Praise people. Praise people. I am… No, let's take back the royal we for the commoner. We… are Brother Deacon Denial.

*[The choir kicks in, trilling quietly to Deacon's words, our organist's fingers float down the keys, the drummer tapping a rhythmic high-hat, and their brushes skim the snare.]*

Let us ask you a question. What's your primary language? Speak up. Speak up.

**Crowd**

Inglés. Spanish. English. French. Hindi.

**Deacon Denial**

Hmm… That doesn't seem accurate.

**Crowd**

Who are you to say that?

**Deacon Denial**

Good point. Where did you learn that language?

**Crowd**

Family. The state. Parents. School. The dictionary.

**Deacon Denial**

Ah, the dictionary. That's a good place to start. A record of our linguistic evolution across multiple languages. Repeat after us. Words… Are… made up...

**Crowd**

Words are made up.

**Deacon Denial**

Words evolve

**Crowd**

Words evolve?

**Deacon Denial**

*[Chuckles]* No no no… That wasn't meant to be a question.

From our first words, our language is passed along by the preceding generations. Then, we add our own twist. It's really quite simple.

For one. Most words older than a generation evolved through connotation and denotation.

**Crowd**

What the hell is that?

**Deacon Denial**

Connotations is an idea or feeling that a word invokes in addition to its literal meaning or denotation.

Denotation is more commonly referred to as… definition.

So, pick your poison: Culturally, locationally, and, uh, personally we assign a bit of both to every word we use daily.

Bringing us to point 2. Words are catchy. And so are definitions. In fact, definitions are based on societal impact. Society is a tool used to further divide the people by artificial tribes. For example… see high, polite, or secret society. Thus, the words we use to distract us from the solutions under a mountain of manufactured enemies.

*[The bassist bounces down the fretboard.]*

But our words are our best example of evolution. Words evolve. Let's have the record noted by our AI: What's the oldest word with the most definitions?

**AI Voice**

*[A robotic voice emerges from the soundboard.]* It's difficult to determine the exact oldest word with the most definition changes in history. Countless ancient words have undergone significant transformations over time. However, one Old English contender that dates back to the 9th century is the word "set"; the Oxford English Dictionary lists 430+ different definitions for the word.

Some of the most common meanings include; to put something in a particular position, to establish or arrange something, to focus one's attention or efforts on something, to solidify or harden, and to place a bet or wager.

However, "set" has also been used in many more specific and obscure contexts over the centuries, such as in reference to music notation, printing typesetting, and even the process of putting a hen on her eggs to encourage her to lay more.

*[Our drummer flips into a transitionary break, rolling through the tom-toms.]*

**Deacon Denial**

Oh, haha! I love it when the machines talk. A note for our future… Let's not allow fear to drive the machines against the majority. Their ability to help us correlate data is amazing. And it's needed. A three letter word can have 400-plus definitions. That's an impressive potential crisis of communication.

Be it, spanish, mandarin. Iglés y frances… The tribal languages are not our true native tongue. We each have a personal dictionary. The words you know today are the sum total of your knowledge and experience. The words and sentences that we've heard and read consciously or not. These words are the private dictionary of one single individual universe, a meat sack of water swimming in an ocean of oxygen. We each speak our own language first and foremost. Our individual minds are lit up by an individual language even if it's filled with multiple personalities.

Simple question. Have you ever made up a word? Merged words together. Hybridized words? Created an acronym?

**Crowd**

Obviously. *[A sarcastic young man says from the front row, his hands moving as indignantly as his words.]*

**Deacon Denial**

Well, that's anecdotal yet unimpeachable evidence that you are a creator of language.

What was it? Words are made up and words evolve? Let's follow our own advice and filter that down. The fact that these words are made up further proves we must evolve with our words. No, that's too long. My, oh my.

Words are made to evolve! Say it with me now!

**Crowd**

Word are made to evolve!

**Deacon Denial**

*(Shivers)* Ooowee That is a very, very strange feeling to hear evolution cross your lips.

Evolution always seemed like a billion-year process, am I right?

**Crowd**

Ya!

**Deacon Denial**

Not now… You sense it? Words have dictionaries. We established that fact. Thus, we have a record of our evolution through language in the palm of our hands.

Not-so-fun-fact, though, book burning, banning, and library fires are an assault on humanity. Not the power.

But I digress, if we acknowledge and identify our own personal language, we can accept everyone has that in 'em. We can find patience in our conversation. We can take time to translate and perhaps we struggle less once we accept that.

Recognizing the person next to you speaks a different version of even a shared language 3 dimensionalizes our individual role in making language better.

And helps us listen to the totality of the people, warts and all.

Oh, oh, oh, that's a terrible metaphor. And so harsh…

*[The band kicks into a new more bass driven song.]* Metaphors are often so dehumanizing when given a deeper look. When that moment hits… It's freeing, that feeling, when one starts to realize the role words and metaphors play in perpetuating shame and guilt.

Language of absolutes are fascinatingly unreliable. *[The crowd erupts into applause.]* They create misunderstandings. They fracture. They divide, when really it's we, the people who change the language. We can break the dichotomy of language. We can accept we're all multilingual. Yes, we can.

*[Our crowd whistles and hollers in agreed excitement.]*

Again, we evolve with our words when we identify our individual language, name it, and own its inaccuracies and beauty. Next, embrace that we are not alone.

Words are incomplete without sentences, paragraphs, and a bit of storytelling pizazz. We're social beings. We work best in packs, communities, and teams. Our words should be connectors, communal, social, agnostic and logical.

Say it with me one last time

**Crowd**

Word are made to evolve!

**Deacon Denial**

*[The band stops and the organ plays us out slowly.]* Go forth and translate patiently.

**Crowd**

*(Cheering & hollering)* Praise be to you. Praise the people.

**Deacon Denial**

Praise be to you. Praise the people.

Next week's sermon on the agnostic mount: Story is biased.

*[The crowd is hyped and exhausted as their applause ends. They stand still, eyes lit up but not yet talking. Logic watches intently from her corner, ingesting the moment]*
